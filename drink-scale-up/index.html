Overview

Taking beverage recipes from benchtop to factory production

A recipe that's right on the bench still has to survive the move to a factory tank.

Scale-up is the stage between a finished recipe and a finished product: translating something proven in small batches into a process a factory can run reliably, at volume, batch after batch. It follows on from beverage formulation and drink recipe development, once a recipe is settled and ready to be tested against real production equipment.

Heat, time, mixing speed and tank size all behave differently at commercial volume than they do in a jug or a small trial batch, and each one can shift flavour, texture or stability if it isn't planned for. Scale-up work is about identifying those shifts before a factory run, not during one.

What tends to go wrong

Challenges when scaling a drink recipe

Most scale-up problems are predictable, if someone's looking for them.

Flavour

Maintaining flavour and aroma profile at scale

Heat exposure and longer processing times can dull or shift a flavour profile that tasted right on the bench, so the process has to be planned around protecting it.

Chemistry

Managing pH, Brix and microbial stability

Keeping acidity, sugar content and microbial safety within the range the recipe and process were designed for, batch after batch.

Physical stability

Avoiding separation and sedimentation issues

Ingredients that stay suspended in a small batch can settle or separate once a product sits for longer, or moves through larger equipment.

Process fit

Meeting factory filling and processing parameters

Making sure a recipe's viscosity, particulates and fill temperature actually suit the filling line and processing equipment a chosen factory runs.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between benchtop formulation and scale-up?

Benchtop formulation is developing the recipe itself, usually in small quantities. Scale-up is the separate work of translating that recipe into a process a factory can run at commercial volume, without the flavour or stability drifting.

Why does a drink taste different when made in a factory?

Usually because something in the process changed along with the volume: longer heat exposure, a different mixing method, or ingredients behaving differently at scale. Planning for this in advance is most of what scale-up work involves.

Do I need to be present during factory trial runs?

Not necessarily, but many brands find it valuable to be there, or to have someone representing the recipe in the room. This gets agreed on a project-by-project basis, depending on what the trial needs.

How do you prevent product separation during pasteurisation?

It depends on the product, but it usually comes down to the right combination of formulation (stabilisers, particle size) and process (heat profile, mixing, fill temperature), checked against how the recipe actually behaves at trial scale rather than assumed from the bench.

Can you help us select and brief a co-packer for scaling?

Yes. Once a recipe is ready to scale, Taste Right can help identify a suitable UK co-packer and brief them on the recipe, process and packaging format.
